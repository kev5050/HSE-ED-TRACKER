    What these numbers are

    The count of patients who have been seen in an emergency department, need to be admitted, and are waiting on a trolley for a bed. It includes trolleys in the ED and on wards. The HSE takes this count at 8am every day.

    "Usual" is that hospital's average over the previous four weeks. Weekends are usually quieter, so weekdays can read as busier than usual until there's more history to compare like with like.

    What they aren't

    This isn't how long you'll wait to be seen. Nobody in Ireland publishes that. A high trolley count does mean a department is under pressure, and that usually means longer waits for everyone.

    For minor injuries such as sprains, cuts or suspected simple fractures, an injury unit or out-of-hours GP is often faster. Children under 16 in Dublin should go to a Children's Health Ireland ED.

    Where the data comes from

    The HSE's daily Urgent and Emergency Care report. We read it as soon as it's published and keep the history, which the HSE removes after two weeks. Hospitals that report but have no 24-hour emergency department aren't listed.

    This site isn't run by the HSE. Figures are copied from their report and may contain errors. If you're unsure where to go, phone your GP or out-of-hours GP service.
